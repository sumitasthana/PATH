# Experiment trajectory

Updated 13 November 2026. This is the index for the dated experiment record. Each page keeps the questions, settings, results, evidence links, and decisions together.

## Latest findings

**The two rank measures are the noisiest things in the study, and they are the ones the paper leads with.** With the accuracy gate failed, the five seeds' indicator records were read from R2 rather than retuning on an accuracy number. The measures split in two. What nine tasks do to the generator's angle, the distance it moved and its weight norm is twenty to fifty times larger than what the seed does, and dormant units clear it by three to one. Effective rank manages 2.1 and srank 1.6. Section 4.1 survives because it averages five seeds: the fall in effective rank is 4.7 standard errors from zero. Section 4.2 does not, because it was going to use one seed per ordering, and at boundary 9 a difference between two single runs would have to clear 72 percent of effective rank's own value, or 81 percent of srank's, before it meant anything. The same comparison is fine for the weight measures, which need to clear 2 to 3 percent. The fix is seeds rather than tuning, since nothing suggests a different step size would tighten rank: eight more runs at 0.7 hours each takes orders B and C to five seeds. The accuracy gate would never have shown any of this, because accuracy was not what the paper compares.

**Five seeds are done, the stability gate fails as written, and the wording of the gate is part of why.** Mean accuracy over the nine tasks came out at 41.41, 37.60, 39.12, 44.63 and 38.11 percent. Best minus worst is 7.03 points against a target of 3. The runs are not behaving oddly: the standard deviation is 2.89, and the gap between the highest and lowest of five draws from that spread should be about 6.7 points, so 7.03 is what it predicts. The target is the weak part. It is written as a range, and a range widens as you add runs, so the same 3 points demands a standard deviation under 1.77 with three seeds and under 0.97 with ten. A study could pass it by running fewer seeds. That is not a reason to accept 7 either: section 4.2 compares single runs of different orderings, and a seed-only spread of 2.9 points means an order difference under about 6 points says nothing. Before retuning or relaxing anything, the spread of the indicators themselves is being read from the five records already in R2, because that is what the paper compares. The damage arm is clean: task 0 hit exactly 5.00 percent in all five runs.

**The learning rate is chosen, and the sweep says the top two values are a coin toss.** Five short runs, each learning the same three tasks and differing only in the step size the model takes when it corrects itself. 5e-4 came first at 49.33 percent average accuracy, 0.53 points ahead of 3e-4. The study treats gaps of up to 3 points as ordinary run-to-run variation, so first and second place are not really separated. 5e-4 was taken because that rule, highest average wins, was written down before the runs started rather than chosen afterwards to suit them. The more useful finding is where the step size has its effect. Across the five values the first task's score moves by 32.4 points, while the last task, scored right after it is learned, moves by 10.6. So the step size mostly decides how much of the earlier work survives as new tasks arrive, not how well a new task is learned. The old untested default of 1e-3 came last of the five, leaving the first task at 11.1 percent against a 5 percent floor. Five runs at 5e-4 come next, and the seven nine-task runs will take about 9.6 hours rather than the 5.1 first estimated.

**The split change broke the old split as well as the new command, and the two run notebooks are written.** Reviewing the 12 November commit found two defects, both confirmed by running the code. `main.py --dataset tiny_imagenet` died asking the ten-group file for task 19, because the default partition path moved while `dataset_defaults` still named twenty tasks. Worse, the old twenty-group split had become unreadable even when its path was passed explicitly, because `load_partition` built the split it expected from a module constant, so `scripts/norm_control.py` and the paired-study notebooks were broken too and the commit's claim that the paper rows would keep building was wrong. A bare `Config()` also gave Permuted MNIST twenty classes out of ten, which fails silently: the head grows outputs no label can reach and chance is reported at the wrong value. The group size now travels with the partition path instead of a separate constant, both splits load again, and a task cannot ask for more classes than its dataset holds. 318 tests pass. **The three run notebooks now exist.** Notebook 43 is the learning-rate sweep on Colab; 44 runs order_A at five seeds and 45 runs orders B and C, both on RunPod, both measuring the indicators at eleven boundaries and appending an unlearn to every run, which lifts the weight-scale arm from one pair of checkpoints to seven. Records upload to R2 after every boundary, so a dropped instance costs one task rather than one run. No training has started; the compute budget is still the open decision. Details in the [13 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-13).

**The study restarts on a new class split, and nothing from the earlier runs carries over.** The 200 Tiny ImageNet classes now split into ten disjoint groups of twenty instead of twenty groups of ten. Nine groups are learned and the tenth is held out as the in-distribution probe. Chance accuracy for one task moves from 10 percent to 5. The generated classifier changes width with it, so every saved checkpoint from the earlier runs is unreadable by the new model and all thirteen runs of the revised design start from scratch. The code change is done and tested; no run has started, and none will until the compute budget is confirmed. The earlier orderings turned out to be the learn-only prefix of the UnCLe paper's Table 4 sequence 1 rather than a choice made for this study, and three new orderings replace them.

**Two thirds of that rise is the weight scale, and the conclusion survives anyway.** The unlearn shrank the generated weights by 8.4 percent, which was recorded as an open alternative explanation and has now been tested with four forward-pass arms and no training. Growing the post-unlearn weights back to the boundary 8 norm removes 67 percent of the effective-rank rise and 69 percent of the srank rise; shrinking the undamaged eight-task weights by the same factor, and changing nothing else, reproduces 39 and 69 percent of them. **The size claim made on 7 November is therefore withdrawn**: what is left after the norm is held fixed is +0.098 on rank and +4 on srank, which sits inside the range of ordinary late-stream steps rather than outside it, and one seed cannot call that a signal. **The result against the indicators stands for a different reason.** On a model whose task 3 had just fallen to chance, all three still moved the way they call healthy, and a measure that reports improvement because the damaging operation happened to shrink the weights is still not reporting damage. The failure now has a mechanism: both measures divide out the scale of a feature matrix, but the path from weights to features runs through ReLU and through batch statistics held fixed, so **effective rank and srank are partly a readout of how large the generated weights are**, and any comparison between models whose norms differ by several percent is partly a comparison of those norms. **This does not reach the stream results.** Across the five source runs the norm rises by 4.4 to 7.5 percent while the rank fall ranges from 25 to 72 percent, and orders 02 and 03 finish with norm changes of 6.62 and 6.58 percent against rank falls of 72.1 and 25.1, so the weight scale cannot be producing the order-dependence found on 6 November. The norm bites at the unlearn because that step moves it 8.4 percent downward in 100 steps, about fifteen times a task's move and the other way, ending below the untrained generator. The script is `scripts/norm_control.py` and it runs on a CPU. Details in the [8 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-08).

**The weight-reading indicators read healthier on a model that has just been damaged, which is the strongest result yet against them.** The boundary numbering was fixed and the first branch job was run on 7 November: order 01 seed 1, unlearn task 3, stop before task 15, twelve minutes on an A100. The unlearning worked, task 3 falling from 34.8 percent to chance at 10.0 while the other seven tasks held within three points, and by this project's earlier measurements that damage is real, a forgotten task being about six points harder to relearn. **All three indicators then moved the way they call healthy.** Effective rank on the in-stream images rose from 2.9708 to 3.2664, srank from 175 to 188, and dormant units fell from 0.2421 to 0.2379, with the same directions on the held-out probe set and on EuroSAT. That single step gives back 18.6 to 22.6 percent of effective rank's fall across the whole eight-task stream and 28.6 to 36.1 percent of srank's. **It is also, before the weight-scale correction of 8 November, far larger than anything late learning produced**: the last three learning steps moved effective rank by -0.109, -0.183 and -0.004 and srank by -3, 0 and 0, against +0.296 and +13 at the unlearn, while the generator itself moved an ordinary distance of 0.0096 and turned an ordinary 0.559 degrees. So these indicators cannot be reporting damage, whatever else they report. **The weights the generator writes stepped back toward their starting values for the first time in the record**, distance falling from 0.3845 to 0.3617 and angle from 21.559 to 21.187 degrees, which is the first direct evidence for the collaborator's claim that forgetting works by returning toward the start. One seed, and one alternative explanation is still open: the generated weight norm drops from 286.34 to 262.36, below its untrained value of 274.19, and although both rank measures divide out the scale, the path from weights to features runs through ReLU and fixed batch statistics, so the shrinkage could reshape the spectrum on its own. Rescaling the post-unlearn weights back to the boundary 8 norm would settle that cheaply. Details in the [7 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-07).

**Unlearning does not make the next task harder to learn, and the indicators cannot see the unlearning at all.** The paired study's October branch and control jobs were read out of R2 on 7 November; nothing was trained. Each branch starts from a finished eight-task model, destroys one task, then learns task 15; each control starts from the same model and learns task 15 with no unlearning. The forgotten task falls to chance in all six branch jobs, so these are genuinely damaged models. **Task 15 is then learned at the same speed either way**: mean final epoch loss 1.3247 for the controls, 1.2899 after forgetting task 3 and 1.3328 after forgetting task 14, with the three-seed spread wider than any arm's gap from its control. This is the sixth direct measurement saying capacity held, and the first taken after real damage rather than after ordinary learning. Accuracy on task 15 hints the other way, favouring the unlearned arm in two seeds of three by 3.2 and 13.0 points, but the controls' own spread is 16.4 points and the loss curves show nothing, so it is recorded as unresolved. **The weight-reading indicators cannot be compared, because no post-unlearning model exists anywhere in R2** and the observer would skip the moment if one were run: boundaries are numbered by tasks learned, and unlearning does not change that count, so the state right after an unlearn step is never written. Loss progress has no value during an unlearn step either, since the forget branch has no task loss and no image batch. A two-line change to the boundary numbering fixes the first and leaves every existing record numbered as it is. That change was made the same day and the first branch job has since run, so this gap is now closed; the result is the paragraph above. Details in the [7 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-07).

**Two more task orders settle the loss-progress row and take the effective-rank fall apart.** Seeds 0 of `order_02` and `order_03` both completed on 6 November, in 2 hours 16 and 1 hour 46 minutes on an A100. All three orders learn the same eight tasks; five sit at the same position in every order and tasks 3, 5 and 14 rotate through positions 1, 4 and 8, so the five fixed tasks measure noise and the three moving ones carry the test. **Loss progress is a property of the position in the stream, not of the task.** Every one of the three moving tasks now has a reading at all three positions, and all three rise with the position without exception: task 3 reads 0.1509, 0.2777, 0.2869; task 5 reads 0.1426, 0.2493, 0.2905; task 14 reads 0.1169, 0.1862, 0.2182. Those spans of 0.1013 to 0.1479 clear the largest gap among the five fixed tasks, 0.0531, by 1.9 to 2.8 times. The end-of-stream dip read earlier from `order_01` is gone: position 8 is the highest reading for all three tasks, and the dip was task 14, the lowest-reading task, sitting at position 8. So the series rises over the first two positions and then stays flat, it never falls, and the row's verdict of no plasticity loss now rests on a complete rotation rather than on one order. **The larger result was not the one the runs were built for.** All three orders were run at seed 0, so all three start from the identical generator reading effective rank 7.3057 before any training, with the same code and the same images. By boundary 8, `order_03` has fallen 22.6 percent, `order_01` 44.6 percent and `order_02` 71.8 percent, and srank 4.8, 11.0 and 38.8 percent, while the generator travels the same distance in all three, 0.1165 to 0.1196, a spread of 2.6 percent. **The same model moving the same distance reports between a fifth and three quarters of its rank gone, depending only on which task came first.** Under `order_03` there is almost no compression to report: rank drops once on the first task and then holds between 5.84 and 6.07 to the end, and srank goes 227 to 216 across eight tasks. Accuracy does not follow the fall either: `order_03` and `order_01` seed 0 end at the same mean final accuracy of 39.20 with falls of 22.6 and 44.6 percent, and `order_02` falls furthest and ends highest at 44.08. Dormant units are the one indicator that survives the change of order, rising between 33.3 and 43.2 percent across all five runs, a spread of 1.3 times against effective rank's 3.2 and srank's 8.1. One seed per new order, and why task 14 first collapses the rank reading while tasks 3 and 5 first do not is not explained. Details in the [6 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-06).

**The direct probe and the plasticity indicators now read the same models, and they disagree.** Notebook 38 probed all three seeds of `indicator_stream_v1` at boundary 0 and boundary 8, the exact models the indicators were read from, using the 29 October protocol unchanged. On those same models effective rank fell 44.6, 35.0 and 61.2 percent on the same images, and dormant units rose on every seed. **Both indicators move in the direction read as damage while the direct measurement moves the other way.** This also settles the standing caveat that the earlier disagreement was inferred across two different studies; it is now measured on one. The prediction written down before the run, that the indicators would stay flat here because this architecture has no slowly trained classifier to blame, failed: there is no such classifier, and the indicators moved anyway, so the adapter study's own explanation cannot be the whole explanation. Three runs cannot support a correlation, and the rank fall does not order with the probe ratio. **The random-direction control, run the same day, says the fall is not just movement.** Notebook 39 rebuilt each stream's starting generator and pushed it the same distances in directions chosen by nothing. Every point on both seeds is at or above its starting value and none falls below its floor, so the two-point design was not hiding a dip. Ratios repeat across sittings only to about one floor, so read them as roughly 1 rather than as precise numbers. **Random-label fitting now sits on the same models as the rest, and still agrees with the probe.** Notebook 41 repeated the October measurement on `indicator_stream_v1`, five repetitions at both points instead of five and two, 90 runs in 121 minutes. Final training loss on shuffled labels falls 0.5953, 0.7902 and 0.6310 against floors of 0.2672, 0.2580 and 0.2017, clearing them by 2.2 to 3.1 times. Same direction as October on every seed, sizes within 0.1 on two of three. So on one set of models: the direct probe says capacity held, random-label fitting agrees, and effective rank, srank and dormant units all point the other way. **Curvature is now measured too, and the honest answer is that it is not readable at this sample size.** Notebook 42 read the mean Hessian eigenvalue at five points by stepping the generator a small distance each way and watching the loss, 180 measurements, nothing trained. The raw readings rise at the end on all three seeds. **A second seed shows the indicators' absolute level is set by the random initialisation, and most of their movement happens on the first task.** Seed 1 of `indicator_stream_v1` completed on 2 November in 2 hours 15 minutes on an A100. At boundary zero, before any training, its effective rank reads 4.56 on the `in_stream` images against seed 0's 7.42, a gap of 63 percent between two fresh generators. So a reported level such as "effective rank 4.0" means nothing on its own. The fall across the stream is also unstable: 35 percent against seed 0's 46 on `in_stream`, and 15 percent against 46 on EuroSAT. **The three-way agreement across image sets reported for seed 0 does not reproduce**, so it was a coincidence of that seed rather than corroboration. What does reproduce is the direction, and the timing: 80 and 83 percent of the whole stream's rise in dormant units happens on the first task alone, and 90 percent of the generated weights' 21.6 degree turn. The generator itself keeps turning steadily to the end, so it keeps moving while what it writes at the neutral reference code stops moving. Seed 1 also ends with a lower rank, 2.97 against 4.02, and a higher mean accuracy, 44.17 against 39.20 percent, which points the opposite way to the reading these proxies are usually given. Two seeds, so none of this is final. Details in the [2 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-02).

**The plasticity indicators and the direct plasticity probe disagree, on the same held-out task.** Reading seed 0's indicator record shows effective rank falling by 45 to 46 percent across all three fixed image sets, including Tiny ImageNet task 2, which the model never learns, and EuroSAT, which is a different dataset. Dormant units rise by about 6.6 in every hundred at every threshold. Taken at face value that is the signature the adapter plasticity study calls lost plasticity. But the direct probe, recorded on 29 October, teaches a fresh code on that same task 2 and finds it learns **better** after eight tasks, by +3.10 points with an interval excluding zero. **The useful finding here is about the indicators, not about plasticity.** They read the representation the generator offers through one fixed reference code belonging to no task; the probe trains a new code. A narrowed representation at a neutral code does not stop the generator re-expanding when a code is trained for the job. Taken at face value the proxies would have given a confident wrong answer, which matters because the UnCLe paper's plasticity claim and the adapter study both lean on them. One seed against the probe's three, so this is not stated as a result. Seeds 1 and 2 have since shown that the size of the rank fall is set by the random starting point, which weakens the proxy side of this comparison further. The across-study caveat is now closed: the [3 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-03) measures both sides on the same models and finds the same disagreement on all three seeds. Details in the [2 November record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-11-02).

**Three seeds at the settled protocol say new-task accuracy rises by about three points after eight tasks.** Tiny ImageNet task 2 read +2.04, +4.27 and +2.98 across seeds 0, 1 and 2, with eleven repetitions at each probe point and the learning rate chosen on a validation split. **The EuroSAT probe's second pass narrowed the bound and put the earlier gain in doubt.** Choosing the learning rate on a validation split and raising the repetitions to eleven at both points cut the noise floor from 14.66 to 5.67 on EuroSAT and from 6.44 to 2.89 on Tiny ImageNet task 2. The measured changes collapsed at the same time: EuroSAT went from +7.76 to -2.09, and task 2 from +5.76 to +1.18. Neither clears its floor, so there is still no verdict, but the change is now bounded far more tightly than before. Nothing fell past a floor either, so the reading that there is no plasticity loss at eight tasks is unaffected. The gain reported on 17 and 21 October is the part now in doubt, because both of those runs chose the rate by test accuracy. **The apparent plasticity gain was too few repetitions, not transfer and not the rate rule.** A third pass put the training split back to 4,800 images and changed nothing else. Tiny ImageNet task 2 read 57.47 at t = 0 and 59.51 at t = 8, a change of +2.04 against a floor of 2.63. So the split controls the accuracy level, lifting both points by about seven points, but not the gap between them. With the rate rule already cleared by re-scoring, the only remaining difference from the first pass is that it took two repetitions at t = 8 against eleven, and the uncertainty on a two-run average is about 3.3 points, which covers the whole difference. The readings of +6.62 on 17 October and +6.37, +3.01 and +4.86 on 21 October are therefore withdrawn as imprecise rather than wrong: three passes at eleven repetitions put the change at +1.18, +2.04 and -2.09, none clearing its floor. **The recovery probe's two stopping points were the same model, and seed 1 exposed it.** `run_request` takes its step count from `config.burn_in`, while the notebook passed the stopping point to `forget_interval`, which only sets how often state is saved. Every arm at both settings ran 100 unlearning steps. Seed 1 made this visible: all six arms keeping task 14's own code give identical traces at both settings, to four significant figures. This withdraws the finding that the stopping point makes no difference, and the step-6 collapse figure taken from this notebook. The dead-code split, the recovery result and the harder-than-unseen result all survive, because they compare arms within one setting. Both notebooks are fixed. Details in the [28 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-28).

**Seed 1 of the recovery probe agrees with seed 0, and is harder on the attacker.** Task 14 fell from 40.4 percent to exactly 10.00 percent on one class. The dead-code split is 50 to 60 steps against 0, against 38 against 2 on seed 0. The attacker finishes behind a fresh start by 2.8, 1.0 and 15.4 points, where seed 0 had it marginally ahead. The forgotten task is about 6 points harder than tasks never seen, matching seed 0. The 20-image candidate signal does not replicate and is dropped. One run, incomplete at 40 of 42 cells.

**The task-conditioning claim is not established, and author code is public.** The journal abstract links to `visprasadh/uncle`; the inspected commit is `f9f1e218588f4382aac4429393510f782626438a`. Its evaluation leaves the target ResNet in training mode, unlike our route probe, making BatchNorm policy a concrete difference to test. Our probe and Table 8 also differ in dataset and evaluation time. Fixed-code training does not test replacement after training, and small arm differences do not prove equivalence. The detailed [27 October audit](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-27#validation-of-the-task-conditioning-claim) supersedes the earlier broad interpretations. The exact discard driver, checkpoints and result provenance remain unresolved.

**The forgetting step trains the shared generator, not the task code.** The optimiser is handed the trunk and the heads, and the task code is frozen throughout. So the paper's discard baseline and its forgetting method do not contradict each other, and a reading recorded here earlier, that the method aims at an address holding no skill, was wrong. What survives is narrower: the loss drives the generated weights to zero rather than to noise, the forgotten model answers a single class, the three success tests are all passed by a silent model, and the collapse is complete by step 6.

**The discard baseline does not establish survival after UnCLe unlearning.** It is a separate intervention. Its aggregate accuracy cannot establish that the generator ignores task identity, and our sampled random routes do not rule out every alternative route. See the [27 October audit](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-27#validation-of-the-task-conditioning-claim) for the corrected interpretation and required controls.

**The probe was repeated on a task that had room, and the answer held.** Task 3 was first in order 01 and had fallen to 35.60 percent before unlearning, so the probe could not have shown a gain. Task 14 is last in the order and stood at 42.60 percent, and a fresh code reaches only about 30 percent at the largest budget, so there were about 12 points of room. The attacker arm still finished in a tie with a fresh start, by 1.9 and 1.3 points. Task 14 was also harder than the tasks the model had never seen, by 5.0 and 5.6 points, rather than easier. This closes the strongest objection to the negative result. Details in the [27 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-27).

**A strong task collapses just as fast as a weak one.** Task 14 stood at 42.60 percent and was at exactly 10.00 percent after 6 unlearning steps, using 1 of 10 classes with every prediction in that class. The step-6 finding had been measured only on a weak task until now.

**The dead-code split replicated on a second task.** 38 steps of dead time for the arms keeping task 14's own code against 2 for the arms using a new one. Three runs, two tasks, the same split every time. A new observation came with it, not yet a claim: once the old-code arms wake, they reach halfway back in 20 steps against 40 to 60 for a new code, so the old code costs dead time and then pays much of it back.

**The recovery probe ran twice, and found no recovery advantage either time.** A model holding task 3's own code and BatchNorm statistics never relearned the task faster than one starting from nothing. It was 7.3 and 6.5 points behind at the 6-step stopping point across the two runs, and 2.3 and 3.7 points behind at the 100-step one. Task 3 was also no easier than tasks the model had never seen, by +1.5, +1.3, +0.1 and -2.0 points, all inside the tie threshold. This is the second failed recovery attempt after rescaling, and neither establishes that the information was removed. One seed, notebook 24, details in the [26 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-26).

**The probe's own noise is wider than the threshold used to read it.** Two runs of the identical configuration differ by 2.0 points on average across the 24 peak-accuracy cells and by 8.8 points at most. The arms carrying the forgotten task's dead code vary three times as much as the arms using a new code, because their peak sits near the step where they start responding at all. The withdrawn +4.5 BatchNorm figure rested on the single noisiest cell, which reversed sign in the repeat. The few-shot images are seeded; the recovery training is not, and it should be before anything else runs.

**What unlearning destroys is the task's address, not the ability to serve it.** Every arm keeping task 3's own 32-number code spent 40 to 50 steps unable to respond to its input at all. Every arm with a new code started immediately, and so did all eighteen unseen-task runs. The split follows the code alone, across both stopping points and all three image budgets, and it repeated exactly in a second run: 42 steps of dead time against 2. The generator still learns task 3 from a fresh code as readily as it learns a task it never saw.

**The collapse is confirmed directly, and it is complete by step 6.** Immediately after unlearning, at 6 steps and at 100 steps alike, the model used 1 of 10 classes and put 100 percent of its predictions in that class. A single-class answer scores exactly 10.00 percent on a balanced ten-class set, which is what forget-task accuracy reports as successful forgetting. The recovery arms themselves were not collapsed: at their best step every one of them used all 10 classes.

**Two summary lines from that run were withdrawn after checking.** The notebook reported the BatchNorm statistics as worth +4.5 points, computed between two arms that both carry the dead code and driven almost entirely by one cell. The contrast that isolates the statistics gives +0.1 and +2.5 points, both inside the tie threshold, so no measurable recovery value is shown. The collapse table took a minimum that included the measurement before training, so it reported collapse everywhere. Both are fixed in the notebook, and the second run confirms the first correction by measurement: that cell reversed sign.

**The direction result holds on three seeds.** At step 20 the weights are 0.9833 to 0.9919 aligned with where they started, a turn of 7.3 to 10.5 degrees. By step 100 they have turned 49.1 to 54.1 degrees. Seed 0 is the mildest of the three, so statements made from it alone understated the effect. No channel lost its weights on any seed. The accuracy side is still one seed, because notebook 27 ran only seed 0.

**The task is gone by step 20, and the other 80 unlearning steps only make it unrecoverable.** Accuracy on the removed task reaches chance at step 20 and never moves again. At that point the weights are 99.19 percent aligned with where they started, a turn of 7.3 degrees, having kept 85 percent of their length. By step 100 they have turned 49 degrees, which no rescaling can undo. So the unlearning length past step 20 buys irreversibility, not forgetting.

**A model at chance accuracy is not evidence that a task was removed.** From step 40 the model answers the same single class for every test image, which scores exactly 10.00 percent on a balanced ten-class test set, the same as chance. Input sensitivity is the measurement that still works: it falls by a factor of 5.8 million across the run, including through the windows where accuracy is flat.

No channel lost its weights, so the 58.9 percent of channels that went silent on 21 October went silent in activations while keeping their weights. The removed task started at 35.60 percent rather than full accuracy, because it is first in the order and had already lost most of its accuracy to ordinary forgetting. One seed, CPU, notebooks 30 and 31. Details in the [23 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-23).

That same record reviewed our plasticity work against the adapter study in `reference/adapter-plasticity-study`, and then corrected itself. The review's main claim, that comparing against an untrained generator cannot answer a plasticity question, was wrong: at t = 0 the full probe already reaches about 52 percent on ten-class tasks against 10 percent chance. The [22 October EuroSAT plan](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-22) is planned again, not deferred.

**What the t = 0 comparison measures is learnability relative to initialization.** It does not separate transfer from plasticity changes, and it does not rule out deterioration relative to an earlier trained checkpoint. The fix is to add trained reference points at t = 1, 2, 4 and 6 when the sequence is next trained, saving the generator after each task, rather than to replace t = 0. A `relative_contribution` docstring that misdescribed its own denominator has been corrected.

The [20 and 21 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-21) finishes the plasticity probe on three seeds.

**The generator learns an unseen task better after eight tasks than before any task,** by 4.74 points on average. Read this as: after eight tasks the generator performed better on the tested probes than a freshly initialized generator, under the chosen training budgets. It does not rule out deterioration relative to an earlier trained checkpoint, and it does not separate transfer from changes in plasticity.

Two measurements agree. Test accuracy on three unseen tasks rises by 6.37, 3.01 and 4.86 points, a mean of +4.74. Two of the three are bigger than their own noise floor; the third is just inside it. Training loss on randomly labelled images falls by 0.70, 0.76 and 0.64, against floors of 0.29, 0.42 and 0.18, so all three pass. Those three values differ from each other by only 0.06, which is very consistent.

That second measurement worked for the first time. On 17 October it was void, because the model never memorised anything. These numbers replace the 17 October reading of +0.8 points, which used one new task, one learning rate and a borrowed noise floor.

Two further results. Training a task's 32 numbers buys nothing. Leave them random and train only the generator. The differences are then -1.48, +1.01 and +1.27 points, against floors of 5.53, 3.23 and 2.28. Separately, a frozen generator with a fresh task code sits at chance on all three seeds. That confirms on three seeds what one seed had shown.

Comparing the three seeds gave two observations, each from three data points. First, one task is far noisier than three tasks averaged. Task 15 spans 16.4 points across the seeds, while the three averaged probe tasks span 2.13. That is why the 17 October spread was 5.5 and this one is 1.68. Second, the seed with the weakest control gained the most from unlearning: controls of 50.8, 44.0 and 34.4 pair with gains of 0.0, +3.2 and +13.0, in that order. Seed 2 supplies most of the +5.40 mean, and its control sits below its own fresh-model baseline. Rerunning that control would settle it.

One defect is recorded. The resume check compared two spellings of the same arm name, so two of three arms were never skipped. They ran again every session. That cost about 3.6 hours and left 276 repeated runs, which are now dropped when the file is read. The main result uses the unaffected arm.

The paper says unlearning restores plasticity. That claim is not supported here, because there is no plasticity loss to restore. The planned beta-zero control arm is no longer needed.

The [17 October record](Experiment-trajectory-2026-10-17) adds the first plasticity measurement and closes it. Plasticity means the ability to learn a new task, which is a separate question from forgetting. Notebook 26 supplies the baseline the study never had: a generator that has learned nothing, taught task 15 alone. Across three seeds with two runs each, the baseline reaches 44.5%, 43.2% and 39.0%, against 50.8%, 44.0% and 34.4% for the same task learned after the eight tasks of order 01. The paired differences are +6.3, +0.8 and -4.6 points, a mean of +0.8 with a sample SD of 5.5. **There is no plasticity change at this probe**, in either direction. An earlier seed-0-only reading of +13.2 points did not survive the other two seeds and is superseded. The planned beta-zero control arm is therefore not needed, because there is no plasticity loss for the protection term to explain. Two further observations: repeated runs of one identical configuration span 8.4 points across sessions, so the 3-point noise floor carried over from the replay work is too optimistic for this measurement; and the eight-task model's new-task accuracy varies three times more across seeds than the fresh baseline's does, which is recorded as an observation and not a claim. This result removes a candidate explanation rather than producing one, and the study's main finding is unchanged.

A teammate's adapter plasticity study asks the same question on different models, and a [read-only snapshot of it](https://github.com/sumitasthana/PATH/tree/main/reference/adapter-plasticity-study) is now in this repository for context. That study trains small adapters, called LoRA and OFT, on a frozen vision transformer and probes whether they keep learning new tasks over 20 tasks. Its provisional answer is that they do, at the learning rates that give the best accuracy. The two projects meet at one claim: the UnCLe paper states that unlearning restores plasticity, no plasticity probe has tested that claim, and the mechanism the paper gives for it, returning a task to a random initialisation, is contradicted by the measurements recorded here. Section 9 of the snapshot's handoff document proposes the shared experiment and records an open decision, namely whether hypernetworks are in scope for the adapter study at all. That decision has not been made, and no shared experiment has been run. The snapshot is a copy of a folder that is not under version control, so it will go stale.
